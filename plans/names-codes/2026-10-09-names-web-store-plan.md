# SimpleX names store: purchase and registration on the web

## Context

SimpleX names (`<label>.simplex`, an ENS fork on Ethereum) get their own store at **simplex.domains**:
1. The buyer searches a name and sees whether it is free, and at what price.
2. The buyer pays by card, Bitcoin or Monero, or redeems a code.
3. The browser makes a wallet for the name, shown as an owner code ("your name code" on the page: `SN-` and 30 characters), and the service registers the name to that wallet's address, paying the fee and gas.
4. The buyer keeps the code. Redeeming it in the app moves the name to the app's wallet.

Nothing is held in custody: the name belongs to the browser's wallet from its first transaction. Badges keep their own store at **badges.simplex.chat**; to the user these are two stores, each linking the other as a secondary option. Both run on the badge service, renamed **StoreService**, and share one logic library.

The registration primitives are Alain's `origin/ab/names-registration-primitives-sponsored` (its plan `plans/2026-10-12-names-sponsored-registration.md`), with simplexmq `ab/name-registration-primitives-sponsored` and ens-contracts PR #35 (`SimplexController`, `SimplexSponsor`, `SimplexResolver`): the owner code, the `Registration` encoding and commitment, the owner's EIP-712 `RegisterName` intent, and a chain client sending `commit` and `registerWithCredit`. This plan is the plumbing and the store around them. The app side (redeeming codes, editing records in the app) is #7530's (`origin/ab/names-actions-api:plans/2026-09-28-name-registration-core-cli.md`). Every flow as a sequence of calls, with the gaps found, is in [`2026-10-13-names-sequence.md`](2026-10-13-names-sequence.md); `e2e/StoreE2E.test.ts` runs the on-chain part against PR #35, from inside a checkout of the PR (`e2e/README.md`).

Facts that shape the design:

- **Registering for any owner:** `makeCommitment` hashes the `Registration` struct rather than `msg.sender`, and `SimplexSponsor.registerWithCredit(registration, nonce, deadline, signature)` registers to the struct's owner once the owner has signed the `RegisterName` intent, with a per-owner nonce the sponsor consumes in order. So the service pays and sends both transactions while the owner is the browser's address; the sponsor spends the registrar's allowance, so the service holds no ETH for fees, only gas.
- **The owner code** is `SN-` and 30 Crockford base32 characters: the wallet's 128-bit entropy and the first 22 bits of its SHA-256. The wallet's key is the BIP-44 key at `m/44'/60'/0'/0/0` of the 12-word BIP-39 phrase with that entropy. A BIP-44 wallet importing the code's BIP-39 phrase holds the name and can edit its records, paying gas; moving it still needs the recipient to claim it, since PR #35's `transferFrom` reverts `RecipientMustBeCaller` unless the recipient or a registrar controller calls.
- **Redeeming in the app** moves the name on `BaseRegistrarImplementation`, EIP-712 domain `SimplexNames`: the code's wallet signs `ApproveName(from, to, tokenId, ephemeralPubKey, viewTag, nonce, deadline)` with `nonce = nonces(from)` (empty key and zero tag: no stealth announcement) and the service relays `approveWithSig`; once it is mined, the app's wallet signs `ClaimName(from, to, tokenId, offer, nonce, deadline)` with `offer = offers(tokenId)` read then (each approval increments it, and `claimWithSig` reverts `NotOffered` on an older value) and `nonce = nonces(to)`, and the service relays `claimWithSig` (PR #35). The registry node follows the token; the records stay. No phrase import is needed.
- **Term:** 2 years for a paid name, the controller's minimum (730 days, `MIN_REGISTRATION_DURATION` in PR #35's `SimplexController`, also in `simplexmq: protocol/simplex-messaging.md`). A code registers for its own term. A year on chain is 365 days (31,536,000 seconds, as simplexmq's `protocol/simplex-messaging.md` prices it), so the duration is `years × 365` days. The term runs from registration.
- **Availability and records** come from the resolver (`simplexmq: scripts/resolver`): `GET /v2/resolve/[<keccak256(label) as 64 lowercase hex, no 0x>].<tld>` → `available | registered{nameRecord} | reserved`. `nameRecord` lists `simplexContact` and `simplexChannel` links.
- **Name rules:**
  - characters and hyphens: `a-z 0-9 -` after folding case (simplexmq's `nameLabelP`, `SimplexName.hs`, also takes A–Z and lower-cases after parsing), each hyphen between a letter or digit on both sides, as `nameLabelP` requires, so no leading, trailing or double hyphen anywhere; #7390's `validLabel` (`origin/ab/names-mvp-edits`, `Names/Protocol.hs`, not merged into the branches this builds on) refuses `--` only at positions 3–4, so it is tightened to this rule with Alain, since a name with `--` elsewhere would sell but never resolve;
  - at least 6 characters from the controller's `minCharLength`;
  - at most 63 from simplexmq's label limit;
  - simplexmq lower-cases a name when parsing it (`SimplexDomain`'s `strP`, `labelHash`) and `validLabel` admits only a–z, so the page folds upper case as typed.
- `.simplex` is not deployed; `.testing` is live on mainnet, but not with PR #35's contracts. The store needs a fresh PR #35 deployment, and a deploy script for it (`e2e/deployLocal.ts` does it for a local anvil chain), since the PR's scripts deploy a plain `PublicResolver`, no `SimplexSponsor`, and a premium price oracle: `ENSRegistry`, `BaseRegistrarImplementation` owning the TLD node (`ENSRegistry.setSubnodeOwner(0x0, labelhash(tld), baseRegistrar)`), `SimplexPriceOracle`, the `SimplexController` proxy initialized with `minCommitmentAge` 60 s, `maxCommitmentAge` 86400 s and `minCharLength` 6 (as the PR's `deploy/simplex/00_deploy_simplex_controller.ts` sets them; the test fixture uses 1 s), `SubnameRegistrar`, `SimplexResolver(ens, subnameRegistrar, controller, 0)` with the controller as its trusted controller, `SubnameRegistrar.setResolver(SimplexResolver)`, `SimplexSponsor` and `setSponsor`, `addController`, `setSubnameHook`, `setMaxLabelLength(63)` (from `deploy/simplex/02`; the fixture leaves it unset), `setDefaultResolver`, `setBeneficiary`, and the guardian's allowance for the registrar.

## The names store, screen by screen

1. **Search** (first screen): the search bar with `.simplex` and Search. A mistake shows the rules line. Search sends keccak(label) and its length, `GET /api/name/:hash?length=7`, and the answer appears on the same screen:
   - available, with its price, or $0 when an applied code covers it;
   - taken, with "Used by" cards, or taken and pointing nowhere;
   - reserved;
   - check failed or rate limited.

   Below it: how it works, what a name does, and the investor card with "Check your code".
2. **Checkout:** the optional links (N3g) and the payment method beside an order card with term (2 years, or the code's), total, Continue, and "I have a code", which opens the code entry. When a code covers the name, the total is $0 and the payment method reads "Paid with your code".
3. **Payment:** the names app's own payment pages (crypto with QR and the held rate, card, and their endings, with "Get a new invoice"), on the shared payment logic; skipped when a code pays.
4. **Wallet and registration:**
   - At checkout the browser generates the owner code, derives the address and the commitment secret from it (`secret = HMAC-SHA256(BIP-39 seed, "simplex.domains commitment " + label)`), and stores them with the name locally.
   - Continue at checkout first shows the code (N3h), paid or by prepaid code: it must be saved, confirmed with a checkbox, before the order is created, since nothing else can recover the name.
   - It sends the address, the name's hash and length, the commitment and `sealed` with the order.
   - After payment the page shows the three steps, ticked as they complete, with transaction links. After the wait the page reveals the registration and signs the `RegisterName` intent, so it must be open then; reopening the link finishes it, and another browser asks for the code (N7b).
5. **Registered:** "Registered for 2 years, until 9 October 2028, to 0x014f…9b73" (a prepaid code's own years when paid by one).
   - The owner code, Copy, and a QR for the app to scan.
   - Next steps: redeem it in the app, point the name at an address, keep the code safe.

**Outcomes off the path:**
- **Taken between search and registration** (by another registrar, or by another order here that registered first): the order keeps its value.
  - Paid: the buyer can register another name at most its price, searched on the order's own page (a cheaper one refunds nothing), or take a code for the taken name's length (8+ for longer names) for 2 years, generated by the page; the service stores its hash.
  - By code: the code is released, unspent, for another name.
- **Registration failed after paying:** the service's own retries gave up: "Paid, not registered", and Try again charges nothing. Refunds are manual, by support.
- **A code that does not work:** invalid or revoked, already used, reserved by another unfinished registration, expired, not yet paid, or not covering this name (the name is shorter than the code's length).
- **Payment endings:** the badge screens.

### 2. Core and primitives: codes, label rule, registration

Builds on `origin/ab/names-registration-primitives-sponsored`, which already has the owner code, the `Registration` encoding, the commitment, the `RegisterName` intent and the chain client. Its intent has carried PR #35's nonce since `965046fe1` (`riNonce`, `registerWithCredit((…),uint256,uint256,bytes)`, the `RegisterName(…,uint256 nonce,uint256 deadline)` typehash); the service still checks the nonce against `SimplexSponsor.nonces(owner)` itself.

- **`src/Simplex/Chat/Badges/Code.hs`:** one sum type for the two kinds of `SN` code, so a code's meaning is in its type:

  ```haskell
  data NameCode
    = NCOwner NameOwnerCode     -- "SN-" and 30 characters: the wallet's entropy and 22 bits of its SHA-256
    | NCPrepaid NamePrepaidCode -- "SN<len>-" and 20 characters: pays for one name of len+ letters
  ```

  - `parseNameCode :: Text -> Maybe NameCode` dispatches by the body's length after `SN`, separators removed: 30 characters is an owner code, 21 or 22 (the length digits and 20) a prepaid code; digits after `SN` cannot decide it, since an owner code's body may start with one; `formatNameCode` renders either.
  - **Owner codes** are the branch's `NameCode`, renamed `NameOwnerCode` (`randomNameOwnerCode`, `nameOwnerCodeEntropy`), and its `parseNameCode` and `formatNameCode`, which handle owner codes only, become `parseNameOwnerCode` and `formatNameOwnerCode` under the sum type's dispatching ones (their uses in `tests/BadgeTests.hs` and `WebUIMock.hs` follow); agree the rename with Alain.
  - **Prepaid codes** (`randomNamePrepaidCode`, `namePrepaidCodeHash`): the length (1–2 digits) then 20 Crockford base32 characters, with a Luhn mod-32 check over the length digits and the payload. The length is shown, not trusted: the table row decides. The canonical form is hashed with SHA-256.
- **`Simplex.Chat.Names.Protocol`:** #7390's `validLabel`, tightened to simplexmq's hyphen rule, is the one character and hyphen check (the length is checked separately); its branch is not merged into #7530's or Alain's sponsored branch, so this stage ports the module or lands after #7390; its comment there forbids a second copy, so the service reuses it rather than adding its own.
- **The `Registration` encoding** stays where the branch has it, `BadgeService/Names/Registration.hs`; the names webapp mirrors it in TypeScript with the branch's vectors.
- **Tests:** owner and prepaid code vectors, including that neither parses as the other; vectors shared with the names webapp.

### 3. Service: search, codes, orders

- **`[names]` in the ini:** `resolver_url`, and `tld` (`testing` until `.simplex` is deployed). `knownSettings` in `Config.hs` gains `[names]`, `[chain]` and the `[listener]` keys `names_static_dir`, `names_export_dir` and `names_hosts`, since the parser warns about and ignores any key not listed.
- **`GET /api/name/:hash?length=N`** (`:hash` as `0x` and 64 lowercase hex, as in request bodies):
  - takes keccak256(label) and the label's length, never the label, refusing a length outside 6–63 with `invalid_name`, and asks the resolver by hash, under its own rate limit rather than the read limit the invoice long poll uses;
  - for a registered name, opens each `simplexContact`/`simplexChannel` link with the chat core's `APIConnectPlan` (`WebEnv` has no chat controller today, since `serviceLanes` builds it without `cc`, so `cc` and the service user's id are threaded into it); `APIConnectPlan` returns the link's display name and picture without connecting, for short links, and a full link has no profile, so its card shows the link only; the fetch has a 3 s timeout, its own rate limit and a short cache, and adds a deep link;
  - answers `available{price, currency, years, resolver, sponsor, chainId, tld}` (the resolver contract the page puts in its commitment; the sponsor and chain id for the intent's EIP-712 domain; the TLD for the records' node), `registered{entities, records, owner, resolver, recordsNonce, chainId, tld}` (`records` the raw `{contact, channel}` values, read from the chain as `text(node, key)` on the configured default resolver with `node = keccak256(namehash(tld) ‖ labelHash)`, because the resolver splits and trims values and answers `""` for a reverted text read, which would make Edit links drop links; reading them raw also keeps a link whose card could not be fetched; they are read only when the answer's `resolver` is the default one, and a name on another resolver or on none (PR #35 allows either without data) answers `records` and `recordsNonce` null with its `owner`, and Your names offers no Edit links for it (the lag answer, with no `owner`, is not this case: Edit links takes the values from the order's links in `sealed`, or from this browser's own last mined edit of a key when it has one (a mined clear counting as an edit of both keys to ""), so a second edit in the lag does not bring back removed links); `owner` and `resolver` from the resolver's answer, which reads them for the hashed node; and `SimplexResolver.nonces(owner)`, read from the default resolver under the same condition, for edits by that owner) or `reserved`. The resolver's 502 `labelNotRecorded` (a registered name whose label the base registrar never recorded) cannot occur on PR #35, where every registration records its label; search would answer it `provider_unavailable`, as it answers the resolver's other errors (502 `upstreamError` for a failed status, owner or resolver read or a lost RPC connection (a reverted text or address read it answers empty instead), 400 `tldNotConfigured`, 502 `unknown` without the registrar address, and 502 `noPriceOracle` without a readable price oracle or premium), a resolver that does not answer, and a failed chain read. The order, `/api/name/register` and the `/name` retarget, which also ask the resolver by hash, answer the same resolver failures with `name_check_unavailable` (503) and create or change nothing, since the badges client reads `provider_unavailable` from invoice creation as an unavailable payment method (N3a); the page shows N3d at checkout, and the error with the reference on the order page. For a hash the resolver reports available while a registration here is `registering`, or `registered` with `registered_at` within the last hour (a margin for the resolver's node lagging the chain, since the resolver itself reads `latest` with no cache; after that the resolver is believed, so an expired name is free again), search answers `registered{entities: [], tld}` with no owner, and Edit links takes the nonce, chain id and resolver from the registration view instead, offered only once the phase is `registered` (the view's `recordsNonce` is null before); once the resolver reports it registered, it is answered like any other registered name, so its links show and Edit links has the owner's nonce.
- **The link bound:** 4096 bytes of UTF-8 per record value (a key's links joined with `;`), checked by the page, by `/api/link/preview` for one link and by `/api/name/records` (`invalid_link`), and by the reveal (`bad_request`), since the service pays for each record's storage: a new 4096-byte value takes about 129 storage slots, some 2.85M gas, against about 65k for its calldata and 33k for the `TextChanged` event, so the register's gas limit, which the branch's client takes from `eth_estimateGas`, reaches about 6.7M for two records at the bound (estimated on the local anvil deployment: 446k with no records, 3.55M with one, 6.67M with two, plus about 30k when the owner's intent is checked; the gas used is about 5% lower near the bound, 10–14% lower for short or no records), and its cap must allow that. Short links take about 70 bytes and the apps' full contact and channel links about 300–400; a full contact address made with `pq_ratchet=on` in the CLI takes about 2.1 KB, so two of those in one record exceed it.
- **`POST /api/link/preview {link}`:** checks the link's kind and the link bound (the page also checks the bound before building the commitment, since the links are fixed in it), then opens a SimpleX contact or channel link with the chat core, as for taken names, and answers `{type, displayName, image}` or `invalid_link`; same timeout and cache, and its own rate limit.
- **`POST /api/code/check {code}`:** for prepaid codes; an unknown code is `code_invalid`, with status 400 since the transport reads every 404 as `not_found`; otherwise returns what a code covers (`minLength`, `years`) and whether it can still be used (`unused`, `reserved`, `used`, `revoked`, `expired`, `unpaid`), without spending it. It uses its own rate limit, since it confirms codes.
- **Prices:** `Catalog.namePrice :: [NamePrice] -> LabelHash -> Int -> Either CatalogRefusal CurrencyAmount` (the label's hash and length), over a seeded insert-only `name_prices` table (`Catalog.defaultNamePrices`): per-length rules, overridable per name by the label's hash. Checkout reprices and refuses a changed price with `catalog_changed`, which now carries the new price.
- **Orders:**
  - `POST /api/invoice {kind: "name", labelHash, length, price, method, owner, commitment, sealed}` (`sealed` null for an order opened from the app, and so in its view) refuses a length outside 6–63 with `invalid_name`, and with `name_taken` a name the resolver reports registered or reserved, or a hash the resolver reports available while a registration here is `registering`, or `registered` with `registered_at` within the last hour (a margin for the resolver's node lagging the chain, since the resolver itself reads `latest` with no cache; after that the resolver is believed, so an expired name is free again); answers an order whose commitment already has an open invoice with that invoice when its owner, hash, length, price and method match (a resend after a lost response), and refuses it otherwise with `400 {"error": "bad_request", "invoiceId": "…"}`, so one commitment never has two invoices open and the page opens the existing order (a buyer who changes the method after a lost response cancels it there); then creates an invoice, and settlement moves the registration to `queued`. Get a new invoice from an ending where the old invoice may still be open (the card form not loading, or the payment details unavailable; a declined card stays in the form on the open invoice) first cancels it through `POST /api/invoice/:id/cancel` (create limit), and makes the new order only after the cancel succeeds: `409 funded` keeps the old order, `409 not_open`, a `200` `paid` (settled during the cancel), or a `200` `expired` with payment marks (a payment read during the cancel) re-reads it: paid with its registration past `unpaid` shows its order; paid with it still `unpaid` (paid after it closed) goes on to the new order; otherwise one holding a payment keeps the old order as `funded` does; and one expired with nothing received (as a successful cancel leaves it) goes on to the new order. On a `200` still `open` the page shows `CANCEL_STILL_OPEN` and keeps the old order, and `503 provider_unavailable` keeps it and shows the error (Stripe may refuse to cancel an intent already processing; that arrives as this `503`, and the invoice stays open until the intent succeeds or the overdue sweep closes it, since `signalOf` reads `processing` and a failed payment's `requires_payment_method` as open; once `settleWindow` and an hour of refused cancels have passed, the sweep marks it expired locally without cancelling at Stripe, and an intent that succeeds after that is a late payment, refunded). The re-read is new: the badges webapp's `main.ts` shows `CANCEL_NOT_OPEN` on `not_open` and checks only a `200` answer. Cancel judges `funded` only from payments the service has read, so a crypto payment not yet read is invalidated with the invoice and becomes a refund case, which the poller already alerts on ("expired holding … needs a refund"), and no twin registration results; the badges webapp's `newInvoice()` only clears the session today. The service checks for a repeat right after the rate limit: a registration whose `name_prepaid_code_id` is this code, with the same owner and commitment (in any phase, the code spent or not), answers that `registrationId`, so a lost response is safe even once its own registration makes the name look taken. The page builds `commitment = makeCommitment(Registration{label, owner, duration, secret, resolver, data = one setText call per key that has links, its links joined with ";" (empty without links), reverseRecord = 0, referrer = 0})` with `secret = HMAC-SHA256(BIP-39 seed of the owner code, "simplex.domains commitment " + label)`. This is the contract's struct, as the branch's `registrationTuple` encodes it from its `Registration` (label, TLD, owner, duration, secret, resolver, contact and channel links); `duration` is 2 years, or the prepaid code's years, at 365 days a year. Its `data` is the records' `setText` calls.

The service stores it unread, refusing one longer than 11095 characters (`bad_request`): the base64url of the 12-byte IV, 8293 bytes of JSON for a 63-character label and two 4096-byte values (SimpleX links need no JSON escaping) and the 16-byte tag, and returns it in the registration view, so a browser that has only the code can rebuild the commitment and reveal. Today's body cap, `maxBodyBytes = 8192` in `Web/Server.hs`, would refuse such an order before any name check, so `/api/invoice`, `/api/name/register`, `/api/registration/:id/name` and the reveal read bodies up to a new `nameBodyBytes = 16384`: a longest order is about 11.4 KB and a reveal with two records at the link bound under 9 KB.

It checks that the label is valid, hashes to `labelHash`, has `length`, so its price is the registration's `price_id` row, fixed at order or retarget (or it is covered by the prepaid code), that each link is a SimpleX link of its kind and within the link bound, that the resolver is the default one, and rebuilds `data` and the commitment; then `verifyRegisterIntent` (owner signed, deadline not passed, secret 32 bytes), plus a low-s check of its own, since `recoverPublicKey` accepts high s and the sponsor's `SignatureChecker` rejects it (the signature is read as exactly 65 bytes `r ‖ s ‖ v` with `v` 27 or 28, as OpenZeppelin requires, the recovery id being `v − 27`), and `nonce == SimplexSponsor.nonces(owner)`, read from the chain. Otherwise `bad_request`, and the registration stays `committed`. The intent does not sign the duration, so the service sets it from what was paid.

The page derives the owner from the code and signs `simplex.domains find <owner> <timestamp>` (EIP-191; `owner` as lowercase `0x` hex, `timestamp` in decimal Unix seconds; the signature read as the reveal's, 65 bytes with `v` 27 or 28) with its key; the service checks the signer is `owner` and the timestamp within 10 minutes, and returns `{registrations: [{registrationId, invoiceId, phase, createdAt}]}`, newest first (`invoiceId`, the order link's id, null for a prepaid-code registration), one for each registration of that owner, retargeted ones included, or `not_found` when there is none. - **`POST /api/name/records {labelHash, key, value, nonce, deadline, signature}`** (or `{labelHash, clear: true, nonce, deadline, signature}`): changes one link record later. The records rate limit applies to every request, before any other check. `key` is `simplex.contact` or `simplex.channel`, each link must be a SimpleX link of that kind (`invalid_link`), and `deadline` is when the page signs plus one hour. A record holds its links joined with `;`, so removing one of several links rewrites the joined value, and clearing a key sets it to "". The page signs EIP-712 `SetText(bytes32 node,string key,string value,uint256 nonce,uint256 deadline)` under the `SimplexResolver` domain with the name's key; `nonce` is `SimplexResolver.nonces(owner)`, one counter for all of the owner's edits, which `GET /api/name/:hash` returns for a registered name (and the registration view while search lags) with the chain id. For the clear form the page signs EIP-712 `ClearRecords(bytes32 node,uint256 nonce,uint256 deadline)` instead, under the same domain and nonce, which wipes every record of the name at once through `clearRecordsWithSig`. The service relays `setTextWithSig` (or `clearRecordsWithSig`), one transaction each, paying the gas, through the registrar key's one sender, the worker's, so an edit and a commit never collide on the key's nonce. After the key and link checks, the service recovers the `SetText`/`ClearRecords` signer (`recoverAddress`, no chain read; the digest encodes `node` as `bytes32`, which simplexmq's `Eip712Value` on its branch has no constructor for, so it goes through `VUint`, not the hashing `VBytes`), requires low s as PR #35's OpenZeppelin `SignatureChecker` does (simplexmq's `recoverPublicKey` on its branch accepts high s, which would pass here and revert on chain), reading the signature as the reveal does, else `bad_request`. It then answers an identical repeat, looked up by signer and nonce and matched by `body_hash` (below), before any deadline check. For a new edit it requires the deadline ahead of the latest block's time (else `bad_request`), answers `rate_limited` past the per-name cap, requires the signer to be the registry's `owner(node)`, read from the chain with `node = keccak256(namehash(tld) ‖ labelHash)` and the registry `baseRegistrar.ens()`, and the edit's nonce to equal `SimplexResolver.nonces(owner)` on chain; else `bad_request`. No refusal counts toward the cap, which counts `name_record_edits` rows. It inserts the pending row before the edit joins the sender, so a concurrent twin loses the unique insert before any signing. It sends the edit, and answers `{tx}` once it is mined, or `record_reverted` with the revert's name when known: a dry run's revert carries its data where the node returns it (anvil and Hardhat 3.19.0 do; reth is unchecked, gap 5), but a mined revert's receipt has none, and the resolver host's reth offers no `debug_traceTransaction`, so a mined revert is named `SignatureExpired` when its block's timestamp is past the deadline and is unnamed (`revert` null) otherwise. On any revert (an applied edit reverts `InvalidNonce`, or `SignatureExpired` once its deadline has passed) the service first re-reads the nonce and the record, since anyone can relay a signed edit: an edit whose nonce the chain has passed, with the record holding its value (for a clear, both keys reading ""), is answered as mined, with `{tx: null}`, since the applying transaction is unknown (another relayer's, or the service's own with its receipt pruned). After a restart each pending edit resumes: it is sent if it has no transaction yet and its deadline is ahead; otherwise its receipt is polled, its saved raw transaction is rebroadcast while the node does not know it and the registrar nonce is unused, and once the registrar nonce is used with no receipt found, the edit is checked on chain by its nonce and the record: if applied, it becomes `mined` with no hash known to have mined; otherwise it is signed again under a new registrar nonce while its deadline is ahead. One with nothing mined once a block `confirmations` deep has a timestamp past its deadline gets the same re-read: if applied, it becomes `mined` with no hash known to have mined; otherwise `reverted` (`SignatureExpired`), freeing the nonce. Its saved transaction is still rebroadcast until its registrar nonce is used, so later transactions of the key never wait on a gap; mined late, it reverts `SignatureExpired` harmlessly. An identical repeat (the same signed body, matched on any row) waits for the same transaction while it is pending, answers its `{tx}` once mined (`{tx: null}` again for one found applied with the transaction unknown), and answers `record_reverted` again for a reverted edit; it never sends another. Only a body that matches no row is checked as new, and with a nonce whose edit is pending or mined it is `bad_request`. After `record_reverted` the nonce is free again for a new body. The page signs the next edit only after that answer, with the next nonce after `{tx}` and the same nonce after `record_reverted`, since a reverted edit leaves the on-chain nonce unchanged; after any `bad_request` it re-reads `recordsNonce` from `GET /api/name/:hash`, or the registration view while search lags. So edits go one at a time and in order, and a failed one never strands later ones (the contract does not queue intents: a higher nonce reverts `InvalidNonce`). Your names hides Edit links once the registered answer carries an `owner` other than the code's address (after redeeming). PR #35 has no edit credits, so the service bounds edits with its own rate limit and a per-name cap.

A commitment not revealed within the controller's `maxCommitmentAge` lapses to `failed`. From `taken` a paid order goes back to `queued` with its new label hash, or ends `code_issued`; a code order ends at `taken` with its code released. - **Chain reads:** in search, the reveal (not counted as its `bad_request`), the records relay (not counted toward the cap), the registration view's nonces (read only in `committed` for `intentNonce` and in `registered` for `recordsNonce`, null otherwise, so a chain outage never blocks an unpaid order's payment page) and `/retry`, a chain read that fails (the node unreachable or answering an error) answers `provider_unavailable` (503) and changes nothing (a revert that is itself the answer, as `ownerOf` for a name not held, counts as not this owner's; a null receipt counts as not mined (pending or pruned), and once its registrar nonce is used the chain state decides (`commitments` for a commit, `ownerOf` for a register, the nonce and the record for an edit); an RPC error is a failed read like any other). For edits, every re-read the sender makes (after a dry-run or mined revert, an expiry, or a used registrar nonce) is retried until it succeeds, with the edit kept `pending` and its repeats waiting; for registrations, the worker's reads (`ownerOf`, `available`, `commitments`, receipts) are retried with the registration kept in its phase.

- **`POST /api/registration/:id/retry`:** from `failed` only (else `bad_request`), in this order:
  1. A register of this registration that went through, with only its confirmation lost: one of its sent register transactions (every hash is kept, fee-bumped replacements included) has a receipt with `status` 1, or `ownerOf(labelHash)` is the owner (the token id is the label hash; a revert, for a name not registered or expired, counts as not). The registration becomes `registered`, with `registered_at`, `expires_at` from `nameExpires` and its prepaid code spent (if the sweep released it to another registration meanwhile, the operator is alerted), whoever holds the name now.
  2. A prepaid-code registration whose released code is now used, revoked, expired or held elsewhere answers that code error and stays `failed`, `failed_at` unchanged.
  3. Otherwise back to `queued`, at no charge, clearing `failed_at`, `error` and the register's transaction hashes and blocks (step 1 has read them). When `intent_nonce` is no longer `SimplexSponsor.nonces(owner)` it also clears `label`, `secret`, `records` and `intent_*`, so the view's null `label` makes the page reveal and sign again rather than the worker resending a dead intent; the reveal is accepted in `committed` while `label` is null.

  The worker then reads `commitments(commitment)`. A successful register deletes the commitment and a lapsed one keeps its time. So 0 means never committed, not yet mined, or consumed; step 1 rules out consumed while the owner still holds the name, since the commitment covers the owner, so whoever consumed it registered the name to this owner. A consuming register goes undetected only once the name has left the owner (redeemed, transferred or expired) after a register this registration did not send: another registrar holding the signed intent, or the owner's own payable register with the code's secret (gap 22). When it reads 0, with a commit of this registration pending (any of its hashes) the worker waits for that receipt; otherwise it commits again, and a commit that reverts `UnexpiredCommitmentExists` (an earlier one mined meanwhile) moves it to `committed`, with `reveal_after` and `reveal_before` from `commitments(commitment)` and the controller's ages. When it reads non-zero, its `revealBefore` is `commitments(commitment)` plus `maxCommitmentAge`, since `reveal_before` may be null. A live commitment (read before that time) is reused, so the registration goes straight to `committed`, with `reveal_after` and `reveal_before` from `commitments(commitment)` and the controller's ages; since anyone can commit a public commitment again after it lapses, `label`, `secret`, `records`, `intent_*` and the commit's transaction hashes and blocks are also cleared when the commitment's time is not the block time of a commit of ours that mined with `status` 1 (a third party committed it again), so the page signs again and the view shows no commit of ours. With less than `revealMargin` left before `revealBefore` (time for the page's reveal and the register to mine; its value is open, gap 29) the worker does not reuse it: it waits until a second after `revealBefore` and goes on as below. At `revealBefore` itself the commitment can neither register (`CommitmentTooOld`) nor be committed again (`UnexpiredCommitmentExists`), so the worker waits a second. After `revealBefore`, `label`, `secret`, `records`, `intent_*`, `error`, the transaction hashes and blocks, `reveal_after` and `reveal_before` are cleared and then the commitment is committed again with the same secret, since the old intent's deadline has passed: the view's null `label` makes the page reveal and sign again.

### 4. Service: registration on chain

Uses the branch's chain client (`BadgeService/Names/Chain.hs`, `sendCommit`, `sendRegister`): JSON-RPC to an RPC URL, the reth node on the resolver host by #7530's decision D5 (whose compose file runs reth `--minimal`, which likely prunes the hash-to-transaction index and old receipts the worker and `/retry` need; gap 25 in the sequence doc), a dry run (`eth_estimateGas`) of every transaction, EIP-1559 fees, signed by the registrar key. `[chain]` holds the RPC URL, chain id, the controller, sponsor, base registrar and resolver addresses, the registrar key and the confirmation depth.

- **Signing:** `signRecoverable` and `signEip1559Tx` (simplexmq #1887, on `names`), and EIP-712 hashing and signer recovery from simplexmq `ab/name-registration-primitives-sponsored`, a PR into `names`. Both must reach the simplexmq the service builds against; the branch's `cabal.project` still points at a local checkout.
- **Worker**, one per registration, resumable after a restart:
  1. `queued` (paid, or a prepaid code reserved) → `sendCommit` with the page's commitment → `committing`;
  2. → `committed` once the commit's receipt is in, recording `reveal_after` and `reveal_before` from its block time and the controller's `minCommitmentAge` and `maxCommitmentAge` → wait for `reveal_after` and the page's reveal. A commit's outcome (its dry run, or its receipt's `status`) is checked against `commitments(commitment)`, since before the reveal the service has no label: a commit that reverts, in its dry run (nothing sent) or mined with `status` 0, while its commitment is live (`UnexpiredCommitmentExists`) makes the registration `committed`, with the reveal times from `commitments()`. One that reverts with no live commitment (out of gas, or a commitment that lapsed by the read) is sent (again, if it was mined), a second later when the read falls at `commitments(commitment)` plus `maxCommitmentAge` itself, as the retry path waits at `revealBefore`; when the commitment had lapsed, `label`, `secret`, `records`, `intent_*`, `error`, the transaction hashes and blocks, `reveal_after` and `reveal_before` are also cleared before the new commit is signed, as retry does after `revealBefore`, so the page signs again;
  3. `verifyRegisterIntent` and the dry run of `registerWithCredit(registration, nonce, deadline, signature)` on the sponsor (its reverts lead to `registered`, `taken` or `failed` as below), then → `registering` with `register_tx` saved, then the send;
  4. → `registered` at a receipt with `status` 1 and `ownerOf(tokenId) == owner`, + 3 blocks, recording `registered_at` and `expires_at`. A mined transaction can still revert (the deadline passed, the name went in the same block, the allowance ran out): on `status` 0 the worker reads `ownerOf(labelHash)` first (the owner holding it is `registered`: a duplicate or resumed send), then `available(label)` to choose `taken` or `failed`.

  Each phase is published to the long poll. Today's long poll cannot carry it: `holdFor` in `Web/Server.hs` answers a paid invoice at once (it holds only while `?wait=` is not `paid`), the waiters (`Waiters.hs`) track only the invoice status and a payment counter, published by settlement, cancel and the poller's expiry sweep, and `waitForChange` in `api.ts` throws on `paid` as terminal. So a separate phase map, keyed by registration id (a prepaid-code registration has no invoice) and left out of `waitingCount`, which sets the payment poller's cadence (`waiting_seconds` against `idle_seconds`), tracks the registration's phase, published on every phase change, by the worker or the service (settlement, `/retry`, `/name`, `/code`), after its database transaction commits; `GET /api/invoice/:id` takes a `seenPhase` parameter beside `wait` and holds while its status and payment marks are the ones seen and, for a name invoice, its phase is `seenPhase`, waking on a change to any of them; a paid name invoice, whose status cannot change, holds on the phase map alone, so it adds nothing to `waitingCount`, and one that reads `paid` with phase `unpaid` (paid after it closed) is not held; `GET /api/registration/:id` takes `seenPhase` and holds while the phase is the one seen; and the library's `waitForChange` sends `seenPhase` for an invoice with a registration and returns when its status, payment mark or phase changes, treating `paid` as terminal only for an invoice without a registration. A new `waitForRegistration(id, seenPhase)` long-polls `GET /api/registration/:id` the same way. Both stop at `registered`, `code_issued`, `taken` and `failed`, and `waitForChange` also at `unpaid` once the invoice is no longer `open` (expired, canceled, or paid after it closed); the page polls again after the buyer's own action (a retarget, a code, Try again).
- **The branch's "left to the plumbing PR"** is this stage's checklist: verify the intent before sending; check the duration and price against what was paid; commit only a paid order; accept only the default resolver, bound the links and cap the gas limit; validate and lower-case the label; one transaction at a time from the registrar key; wait for the commit's receipt and `minCommitmentAge`; bound RPC responses; read the revert reason from `error.data`; keep the RPC URL out of logged errors; add the modules to the executable; pin simplexmq. This plan adds replacing a stuck transaction with a fee bump after a timeout (gap 3); a request timeout in `rpc`, which calls `httpLbs` with no `responseTimeout`, so a stuck node would otherwise hold a search, reveal or records request for the Manager's default; and keeping `error.code` (3 for a revert), which `rpc` drops today, so a revert such as `ownerOf`'s for a name not held is told apart from a node failure. It also splits the branch's `sendTransaction` into signing (the raw transaction and its hash, keccak256 of the raw bytes) and sending, so each hash is saved before its transaction is sent.
- **`taken`:** the name is no longer available: a register that reverts, in its dry run (nothing sent) or mined with `status` 0, with `NameNotAvailable` or `NameReserved` decoded, or with any other revert while `available(label)` is false, unless `ownerOf(labelHash)` is the owner, which means this registration already went through (a resumed worker or a duplicate send) and is `registered`, recording `registered_at` and `expires_at` and spending its prepaid code (the sponsor checks the intent and the allowance before availability, so a taken name can revert with those first); the payment or prepaid code is kept, and the outcomes above apply.
- **`failed`:** retried; still failing → "Paid, not registered".
- **Records:** the links chosen at checkout, if any, are set by the register transaction: its `data` holds one `setText` call for each of `simplex.contact` and `simplex.channel` that has links, on the name's node, with the links joined by `;` (`LINK_SEPARATOR` in `snrc-resolve.py`). Without them the name is registered with none.
- **Funding:** `registerWithCredit` spends the registrar's allowance on the sponsor, so the registration fee is not paid in ETH; the registrar key pays only gas. A gas spike is the service's loss.
- **Key risk:** the registrar key holds gas and spends an allowance; it holds no names by policy. A leaked key can spend the whole allowance: registering to itself needs no signature, registering to any address it controls needs only that address's signature, and `renewWithCredit` needs none. The allowance is kept small, and the guardian zeroes it as the kill switch.
- **Allowance:** only the guardian (`controller.beneficiary()`) sets it, with `setRegistrarAllowance(registrar, allowanceUSD)` in attoUSD; it replaces, not adds. Each registration spends the name's list price for its whole duration (5 years for a 5-year code). `InsufficientAllowance` alerts the operator; while `available(label)` is true it is a `failed`, never `taken`.
- **Redeeming in the app (later):** the service relays the code wallet's `approveWithSig` and the app wallet's `claimWithSig` on the base registrar; an endpoint for it comes with the app's redeem flow.
