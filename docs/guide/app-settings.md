# App settings

This page is ordered as the app's menus: the profile menu, **Settings** and its
screens, **Advanced settings**, and the app version screen with **Developer
tools**. Settings of a task are described on the task's page, and linked from
here.

## Opening the app settings

1. Tap your profile image in the chat list toolbar. The toolbar is at the bottom
   of the screen by default, and at the top when the reachable toolbar is off in
   [Appearance](#appearance).
2. Tap **Settings** in the profile menu.

The layout of the chat list is described in [Your
chats](./README.md#your-chats).

## Profile menu

Tap your profile image to open the profile menu.

| Row | Details |
|---|---|
| Your profiles | Your current profile and your other visible profiles. Tap your current profile to edit it ([Edit your profile](./chat-profiles.md#edit-your-profile)). Tap another profile to switch to it ([Switching between profiles](./chat-profiles.md#switching-between-profiles)). |
| **Create SimpleX address** or **Your SimpleX address** | [Your SimpleX contact address](#your-simplex-contact-address) |
| **Chat preferences** | [Chat preferences](#chat-preferences) |
| **Your chat profiles** | Adding, hiding, muting and deleting profiles: [Chat profiles](./chat-profiles.md). |
| **Use from desktop** (iOS, Android), **Link a mobile** or **Linked mobiles** (desktop) | [Linked devices](./linked-devices.md) |
| **Settings** | [Settings](#settings) |

On desktop, your other profiles are listed after **Chat preferences**.

Tap the sun or moon icon next to **Settings** to switch between light and dark
mode. Long press it, or right-click it on desktop, to follow the system mode.

### Your SimpleX contact address

Tap **Create SimpleX address**, or **Your SimpleX address** once you have one,
to open your SimpleX address: a link that people can use to connect to you,
which you can share publicly. How to create, share and set it up is described in
[Your SimpleX contact
address](./making-connections.md#your-simplex-contact-address).

### Chat preferences

Tap **Chat preferences** to open **Your preferences**: the preferences for all
contacts of the current profile, such as disappearing messages and voice
messages. Preferences can also be set for each contact. The preferences and
their defaults are described in [Contact
preferences](./send-messages.md#contact-preferences).

### Your privacy

**Your privacy** is divided into the sections **Device**, **Chats**, **Files**
and **Auto-accept**, with the row **More privacy** at the end.

#### SimpleX Lock

With SimpleX Lock on, the app asks you to authenticate when you open it, and
when you return to it after it was in the background longer than **Lock after**.

To turn it on:

1. Open **Settings**, then **Your privacy**, then **SimpleX Lock**.
2. Turn on **Enable lock**.
3. Authenticate with your device, or set a passcode when the lock mode is
   **Passcode**.

| Setting | Details |
|---|---|
| **Lock mode** (iOS, Android) | **System** (default): the device authentication, with biometrics or the device passcode. **Passcode**: a passcode for the app. Desktop uses a passcode. |
| **Lock after** | 10 seconds, 30 seconds (default), 1 minute, 3 minutes, 10 minutes or **Immediately**. |
| **Change passcode** | Shown in the **Passcode** mode. |
| **Allow sharing** (iOS, section **Share to SimpleX**) | While SimpleX Lock is on, you can share from other apps to SimpleX only when this is on. Off by default. See [Share from other apps](./send-messages.md#share-from-other-apps). |

The app offers to turn on SimpleX Lock once, with the alert **SimpleX Lock** and
the button **Turn on**: on iOS when your chat list has more than 2 chats, on
Android and desktop when it has more than 3 chats.

#### Self-destruct passcode

In the **Passcode** mode, turn on **Enable self-destruct** in the section
**Self-destruct passcode**, and set a second passcode and a **New display
name**. The app explains what happens if you enter the self-destruct passcode
while opening the app:

1. All app data is deleted.
2. App passcode is replaced with self-destruct passcode.
3. An empty chat profile with the provided name is created, and the app opens as
   usual.

Tap **Change self-destruct passcode** to set a new one.

#### Protect app screen

With **Protect app screen** (iOS, Android) on, the app screen is hidden in the
recent apps. On iOS it is off by default. On Android it is on by default, and
screenshots of the app are prevented as well.

#### Chats and files

| Setting | Details |
|---|---|
| **Send link previews** | The app loads a preview of the links you send. The app warns: "Sending a link preview may reveal your IP address to the website." On by default. The first time you type a link, the app asks **Enable link previews?**. |
| **Remove link tracking** | The app removes tracking parameters from the links you type. Off by default. |
| **Auto-accept images** | The app receives images, small videos and short voice messages without asking. On by default. |
| **Blur media** | **Off** (default), **Soft**, **Medium** or **Strong**: images, videos and link previews are blurred until you tap them. |
| **Contact requests in groups** (section **Auto-accept**) | The app accepts the requests of group members to talk to you directly. Off by default. |
| **Group invitations** (section **Auto-accept**) | The app joins the groups you are invited to. Off by default. |

The **Auto-accept** settings, and the delivery receipts in **More privacy**, are
set for your current profile.

#### More privacy

| Setting | Details |
|---|---|
| **Show last messages** | The last message of each chat is shown in the chat list. On by default. |
| **Message draft** | The message you started is kept when you leave the chat. On by default. |
| **Verify SimpleX names** | When on, the app verifies the SimpleX name of a contact or a channel when you open its chat info. When off, tap **Verify name** there. Off by default. See [Registering a SimpleX name](./register-simplex-name.md). |
| **Encrypt local files** | The files you receive are stored encrypted on your device. On by default. |

## Advanced settings

### Network and servers

| Section | iOS | Android and desktop |
|---|---|---|
| **Preset servers** | The server operators, and **Review conditions** or **Accepted conditions** | the same |
| **Messages & files** (iOS), **Messages and files** (Android, desktop) | **Your servers**, **Advanced network settings** | **Your servers**, **Use SOCKS proxy**, **SOCKS proxy settings**, **Advanced network settings** |
| Saving | **Save servers** | **Save servers** |
| **Calls** | | **WebRTC ICE servers** |
| **Network connection** | The network type, with a green dot when the device is online | Android only, the same |

Changes to the servers are saved with **Save servers**. If you leave with
unsaved changes, the app offers to save them.

The WebRTC ICE servers are also in **Audio & video calls** on all platforms: see
[Audio & video calls](./audio-video-calls.md).

#### Server operators

The app has two preset server operators: SimpleX Chat and Flux. In onboarding,
under **How it helps privacy**, the app explains: "The app protects your privacy
by using different operators in each conversation. When more than one operator
is enabled, none of them has metadata to learn who communicates with whom." When
the app chooses a server, it prefers servers of operators that the conversation
does not use yet.

Tap an operator to open its servers:

| Row | Details |
|---|---|
| Operator logo | **Network operator**: who runs the servers, and the operator's website. |
| **Use servers** | The operator's servers are used. If the operator's conditions of use are not accepted yet, the app shows them with **Accept conditions**. |
| **Chat relays** | The operator's chat relays. "Chat relays forward messages in channels you create." See [Chat relays](./channels.md#chat-relays). |
| **Use for messages** | The roles of the operator's message servers: **To receive**, **For private routing** and **To resolve names**. |
| **Message servers** | The operator's message servers. |
| **Use for files** | The role of the operator's file servers: **To send**. |
| **Media & file servers** | The operator's media and file servers. |
| **Test servers** | A test of the connection to each server. |

When the conditions of use changed, **Review conditions** is shown. Tap it to
see them, with **Accept conditions** and, when set, the date when they will be
accepted automatically for the enabled operators. Tap **Accepted conditions** to
see the conditions you accepted.

Server operators can block a link or a file that violates their conditions of
use. When a link is blocked, the app shows **Connection blocked** with the
reason given by the operator. For a blocked file, the app shows "File is blocked
by server operator" with the reason. Tap **How it works** to read [an
explanation on the SimpleX
blog](../../blog/20250114-simplex-network-large-groups-privacy-preserving-content-moderation.md#preventing-server-abuse-without-compromising-e2e-encryption).

#### Your servers

In **Your servers**, the app shows the servers you added: **Chat relays**,
**Message servers** and **Media & file servers**. The message and file servers
are "the servers for new connections" and "for new files" of your current chat
profile.

- Tap **Add server** and choose **Enter server manually**, **Scan server QR
  code** (iOS, Android) or **Chat relay**.
- Tap a server to test it with **Test server**, or to turn **Use for new
  connections** on or off. For a message server you added and use, you can also
  set **To receive**, **For private routing** and **To resolve names**. For a
  server you added, a QR code is shown under **Add to another device**.
- Tap **Test servers** to test all servers.
- Tap **How to use your servers** to open the guide to [hosting your own
  server](../SERVER.md).

#### SOCKS proxy

The app can connect to servers through a SOCKS proxy, for example a proxy to the
Tor network provided by the Orbot app.

| | iOS | Android and desktop |
|---|---|---|
| Where | **Advanced network settings**, section **SOCKS proxy** | **Network & servers**: **Use SOCKS proxy** and **SOCKS proxy settings** |
| Address | **IP address** and **Port**. The host must be an IP address. | **Host** (default localhost) and **Port** (default 9050) |
| Credentials | **Proxy requires password**, with **Username** and **Password** | **Proxy authentication**: **Use random credentials** (default), or a username and a password |

On Android and desktop, when you turn on **Use SOCKS proxy**, the app asks **Use
SOCKS proxy?** and notes that the proxy must be started before you enable this
option. If the network timeouts are at their defaults, the app sets longer
timeouts for the proxy. While the proxy is used, the app notes: "message and
file relays are connected via SOCKS proxy. Calls use direct connection." When
you turn it off, the app asks **Use direct Internet connection?**: "If you
confirm, the messaging servers will be able to see your IP address, and your
provider - which servers you are connecting to."

#### Use onion hosts

With **Use .onion hosts**, you choose whether the app connects to the .onion
addresses of servers: **No**, **When available** or **Required**. With
**Required**, the app connects only to servers that have a .onion address.

- iOS: in **Advanced network settings**. **No** by default. The app notes that
  **When available** and **Required** need a compatible VPN.
- Android and desktop: in **SOCKS proxy settings**. **When available** by
  default. It can be changed while the SOCKS proxy is on and **Use random
  credentials** is on; otherwise the app does not use .onion hosts. The app
  notes: "Set Use .onion hosts to No if SOCKS proxy does not support them."

#### Private message routing

In **Advanced network settings**, section **Private message routing**. The app
explains: "To protect your IP address, private routing uses your SMP servers to
deliver messages."

| Setting | Options |
|---|---|
| **Private routing** | **Always** (default): always use private routing. **Unknown servers**: use private routing with unknown servers. **Unprotected**: use private routing with unknown servers when IP address is not protected. **Never**: do not use private routing. |
| **Allow downgrade** | **Yes**: send messages directly when your or destination server does not support private routing. **When IP hidden** (default): only when your IP address is protected. **No**. |
| **Show message status** | An arrow → is shown on messages sent via private routing. Off by default. |

iOS shows the options in lowercase: **always**, **unknown servers**,
**unprotected**, **never**; **yes**, **when IP hidden**, **no**.

#### Transport isolation

With **Transport isolation**, you choose when the app uses separate TCP
connections:

| Mode | Details |
|---|---|
| **Chat profile** | A separate TCP connection (and SOCKS credential) for each chat profile you have in the app. |
| **App session** (default) | As **Chat profile**, and new SOCKS credentials every time you start the app. |
| **Server** | As **Chat profile**, and new SOCKS credentials for each server. |
| **Connection** | A separate TCP connection (and SOCKS credential) for each contact and group member. The app warns: "if you have many connections, your battery and traffic consumption can be substantially higher and some connections may fail." |

iOS shows **Transport isolation** in **Advanced network settings** after you
turn on **Show developer options** in [Developer tools](#developer-tools).
Android and desktop always show it there, and offer **Connection** after you
turn on **Show developer options**.

#### Advanced network settings

<img src="./images/app-settings-advanced-network-settings.png" alt="Advanced network settings on iOS" width="300">

Besides the settings above, **Advanced network settings** contains:

- **TCP port for messaging**: **Use web port** with **All servers**, **Preset
  servers** (default) or **Off**. With **Preset servers**, the app uses TCP port
  443 for preset servers only. With **All servers**, it uses port 443 for
  servers without a port, and with **Off** it uses port 5223.
- **TCP connection**: **TCP connection timeout**, **TCP connection bg timeout**,
  **Protocol timeout**, **Protocol background timeout**, **Protocol timeout per
  KB**, **PING interval**, **PING count**, **Enable TCP keep-alive** with
  **TCP_KEEPIDLE**, **TCP_KEEPINTVL** and **TCP_KEEPCNT**.
- **Reset to defaults**.
- **Set timeouts for proxy/VPN** (iOS): longer timeouts for connections through
  a proxy or VPN. On Android and desktop, the app sets them when you turn on the
  SOCKS proxy.
- **Save and reconnect**: the app asks **Update settings?**, as the app then
  reconnects to all servers.

### Notifications

In **Notifications** (iOS and Android), you choose how the app receives messages
while it is in the background. The modes and their effect on privacy and battery
are described in [Notifications](./notifications.md).

### Audio and video calls

The WebRTC ICE servers, **Always use relay** and the call settings of each
platform are in **Audio & video calls**. See [Audio & video
calls](./audio-video-calls.md).

### Shut down the app

On Android, when you tap **Shutdown**, the app asks **Shutdown?**:
"Notifications will stop working until you re-launch the app". Tap **Restart**
to restart the app, or **Shutdown** to stop it.

### App version

The last row of **Advanced settings** is the app version: for example **v7.1**
on iOS, and the version with the build number on Android and desktop. Tap it to
open **App version**, with the app version, app build, core version and
simplexmq version, and then:

- **Developer** (iOS) or **Developer tools** (Android, desktop): [Developer
  tools](#developer-tools).
- **Check for updates** (desktop): **Disabled** (default), **Stable** or
  **Beta**.

With **Stable** or **Beta**, the desktop app checks the releases of SimpleX Chat
on GitHub when it starts and then once a day. When a newer version is available,
it shows **Update available**, the release notes, and **Download**, **Skip this
version** and **Remind later**. When the download is complete, it offers
**Install update**, **Open file location** and **Cancel**; you install .deb
packages yourself from the file location. Once, when you have more than 3 chats,
the app asks **Check for updates** with **Stable**, **Beta** and **Disable**.

### Developer tools

| Row | Details |
|---|---|
| **Install SimpleX Chat for terminal** | The SimpleX Chat repository on GitHub. See [SimpleX Chat for the terminal](../CLI.md). |
| **Chat console** | The commands that the app sends to the chat core, and their responses. You can send commands there; they are run on your chats and data. With SimpleX Lock on, the app asks you to authenticate first. |
| **Reset all hints** | The app's hints and notices are shown again. |
| **Show developer options** | Database IDs and the transport isolation option are shown. |

With **Show developer options** on, the section **Developer options** contains:

- **Confirm database upgrades**: before the database is upgraded for a new app
  version, the app shows **Database upgrade** and waits until you tap **Upgrade
  and open chat**. Off by default: the database is upgraded without asking.
- **Storage**: the space used by the app's folders.
- Android and desktop: **Enable logs**, **Show internal errors** and **Show slow
  API calls**.
- Desktop: **Show console in new window**.

The section **Deprecated options** contains **SimpleX links**. **Description**
(default): the type of a SimpleX link and its server are shown instead of the
link. **Full link**: the whole link is shown.

## Crowdfunding

The section **Own a part of SimpleX Chat** contains the row **Crowdfunding on
Wefunder**. Tap it to open **Invest in SimpleX Chat**, with a link to the
SimpleX Chat crowdfunding on Wefunder. The row is shown:

- on iOS, when the App Store country is the United States or is not known;
- in the Android app from Google Play, when the Google Play country is the
  United States or is not known;
- in the desktop app and in other Android builds, in every country.
